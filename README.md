# CS-250-Software-Development-Lifecycle
Portfolio work from CS 250, including my Sprint Review and Retrospective and reflections on Agile and Scrum practices.
## Reflection

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first trying to understand what the user is actually trying to accomplish instead of immediately focusing on how I am going to program it. User stories help by breaking larger requirements into smaller pieces that explain what the user needs and why they need it. This gives developers and testers a clearer goal to work toward. During the SNHU Travel project, I learned that user stories also make changing requirements easier to manage because individual features can be updated without having to rethink the entire project.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I have learned that I work better when I break a program into smaller pieces and get each part working before moving on to the next one. Instead of trying to build an entire application at once, I can develop, test, and improve individual features as I go. In future projects, I would like to continue using user stories, prioritized backlogs, incremental development, and frequent testing. I also like the idea of retrospectives because they provide an opportunity to look at what worked, what did not, and what could be improved before continuing.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means more than completing your own assigned work. It requires communication, reliability, and being willing to listen to other members of the team. Developers, testers, Product Owners, and Scrum Masters may have different responsibilities, but their work affects everyone else. This course showed me how important it is to communicate when requirements change, ask questions when something is unclear, and share problems early instead of waiting until they become larger issues.
